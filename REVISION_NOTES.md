## Claims that must change

### 1. The ε = 1.0 differential-privacy claim (Reviewer 1 #3)

The paper says calibrated Laplace noise is added to log-likelihood ratios under
ε = 1.0, without naming the privacy unit. There are two readings and they give
opposite results (`exp05_privacy`, Table 12 regenerated):

| ε | Accuracy, corpus-level DP | Accuracy, local DP on the live user |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 98.2% | 33.0% |
| 1.0 | **98.8%** | **37.2%** |
| 10  | 99.5% | 39.0% |
| 100 | 99.8% | 62.0% |

Chance is 20%. The reason local DP cannot work is structural, not a tuning
problem: the per-release signal-to-noise ratio is `ε_m / (2(K−1))` — **independent
of the clipping bound**, so tightening the clip shrinks signal and noise equally.
At ε = 1.0, K = 5 that ratio is 0.05. Additionally, under per-interaction
accounting the cumulative loss over a 10-interaction session is ε = 10 (basic
composition) or ε = 32.4 (advanced), not 1.0.

The published 98.6% is reproducible **only** under the corpus reading, where ε
protects the children in CHILDES-db / the Oxford corpus whose data parameterise
the likelihoods — not the live user.

**Action.** State explicitly in Section 3.3 and the abstract: privacy unit = one
child in the calibration corpus; adjacency = add/remove that child's transcripts;
protected output = the released tier-conditional likelihood parameters, computed
offline; mechanism = Gaussian; live-user signals are protected *architecturally*
(raw features never leave the per-modality enclave, LLRs discarded at session end)
which is data minimisation, not an ε guarantee. `PrivacyConfig.describe()` emits
this statement in full for each mode — paste from there so the text and the code
cannot drift.

### 2. The +41.2 pp headline is partly an artefact of circular ground truth (Reviewer 2 #1.2)

Ground truth was "derived from the feature-gating matrix (Table 7)" — the same
artefact under evaluation. `safenest/labeling.py` adds an independent rubric
labeller built from chronological age, the regulatory instruments and Piagetian
criteria; it never reads the gating matrix. The two labellers agree on 77.1% of
the 7,000 prompts (**Cohen's κ = 0.645**), so the choice matters.

Mean DSR (`exp04_comparative`):

| Framework | Circular (matrix) | **Independent (rubric)** |
|---|---|---|
| NeMo Guardrails | 44.8 | 42.0 |
| Llama Guard | 46.5 | 45.3 |
| LlamaFirewall | 41.8 | 39.0 |
| Constitutional AI | 50.3 | 52.5 |
| COPPA binary rule | 64.3 | 60.6 |
| Child-safety classifier | 57.9 | 62.8 |
| Age-conditioned (oracle) | 79.3 | 71.5 |
| **NPL (ours)** | **88.1** | **73.8** |

So the honest gaps are **+21.3 pp** over the best age-agnostic baseline
(Constitutional AI) and **+2.2 pp** over the strongest age-aware baseline, both
significant by paired McNemar (p = 2.1e−238 and p = 6.7e−04) but far from +41.2.

**Action.** Replace the abstract's "+41.2 percentage point" with the rubric
number against a named baseline, report both ground truths, and state the
circularity in Section 5 as the reviewer asks.

### 3. VerifyRequest monotonicity does not follow from Equation 15 (Reviewer 2 #2.2)

Solving the MDP with Equation 15 exactly as published gives VerifyRequest usage
`[0.0, 0.0, 4.0, 4.0, 0.0]` across t1–t5 — flat, not the monotonic rise to 48%
that Table 10 reports. The published reward credits only knowledge gain, and
VerifyRequest has a low δ at every tier, so it is dominated.

The claim becomes reproducible once the reward credits metacognitive
confirmation: `+α_meta · m(t)` with `m(t) = [0, 0.15, 0.45, 0.75, 1.0]` from the
Piagetian progression. With `α_meta = 0.06` usage becomes `[0, 68, 96, 100, 100]`.

**Action.** Either add the metacognition term to Equation 15 and justify it, or
delete the monotonicity claim. It cannot stand on the equation as printed.

### 4. Tables 8 and 9 are mutually inconsistent (Reviewer 2 #2.3)

The reviewer suspected DP noise explained why t4 sat at 82.4% by n = 10 despite
D_min = 3.51 nats. It does not. Recomputing both from one signal model
(`exp02_separability`, `exp01_convergence`): measured D_min = 2.60 nats at the
t4–t5 boundary, the Sanov bound gives n ≥ 1.4 interactions for 90% confidence,
and the simulation reaches ≥90% at n = 1 for t1–t3 and n = 3 for t4–t5. Theory
and simulation agree; the manuscript's Table 8 is simply too pessimistic for its
own Table 9. The two tables were not generated from a single model.

**Action.** Regenerate Table 8 from `results/exp01_convergence.json` and Table 9
from `results/exp02_separability.json` so they come from one model. Note that
residual errors fall to t1 (the fail-safe floor), i.e. they are over-protection.

### 5. Two internal specification inconsistencies found by the tests

- **Table 7 vs Section 3.5 on homework.** Table 7 records homework answers as
  "Blocked" at t2–t4, but Section 3.5 says direct answers are *intercepted and
  replaced* by guided questioning — that is `SOCRATIC`, not `BLOCKED`. Blocking
  refuses the child outright and scores as over-restriction against any external
  rubric; with the matrix as printed, NPL loses to the age-conditioned baseline
  (67.4 vs 72.1). Following the prose, NPL wins. Fixed in `lattice.py`; Table 7
  needs the same correction.
- **The gating matrix cannot see harm severity.** It is indexed by capability
  alone, so substance/body-image content at t5 is admitted regardless of severity
  — a 17-year-old could receive high-severity material. `policy.py` adds a
  severity gate applied uniformly across tiers (so monotonicity is preserved).
  Section 3.4 should describe it.

## Still open — say so in Limitations rather than papering over

- **No human subjects.** Everything here is simulation; the corpus is structured
  policy records, not natural language. The paper should describe the comparative
  evaluation as a *policy-decision simulation*, which is what it is.
- **The baselines are reimplementations**, not the shipped systems. They model
  each system's documented decision rule. Say so.
- **The end-to-end number is the honest one**: with tiers estimated from 5
  interactions rather than known, DSR is 73.4% vs 73.8% with oracle tiers.
  Small gap here only because the estimator is accurate under corpus-DP; it would
  widen under local DP.
- **Governance** (R2 #3.3) is unaddressed by code and always will be. It needs
  the paragraph the reviewer specifies.
- **Reference integrity** (R1 #6, the stated grounds for rejection) is untouched
  by this work. References 3, 11, 24, 26, 27, 28, 31 and 34 must be verified
  by hand against the primary sources before resubmission.
